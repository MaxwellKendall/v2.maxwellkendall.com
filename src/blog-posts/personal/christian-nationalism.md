**Master Question:**  
Does the civil magistrate’s God-given authority to govern the nations—under Christ’s present kingship—extend to promoting or enforcing explicitly Christian religious ends such as worship, doctrine, and confession, or is his authority limited to administering temporal justice, preserving civil peace, and upholding the natural moral order?

1. Do you agree that Christ is the reigning Lord over all creation, including every nation and ruler?

2. Do you agree that Christ administers His rule through the biblical covenants rather than outside or against those covenants?

3. Which biblical covenant establishes the authority, structure, and limits of civil government for all nations?

4. What responsibilities does God assign to humanity through civil authority in the Noahic covenant?

5. Does the Noahic covenant assign the civil ruler any authority to enforce worship, regulate doctrine, suppress idolatry, or advance redemptive truth?

6. Does the New Covenant expand the civil magistrate’s responsibilities, or does it instead establish a spiritual kingdom administered by Word and Spirit through the church?

7. Can you identify any New Testament passage where Christ expands the magistrate’s jurisdiction beyond the Noahic mandate to enforce Christian worship or doctrine?

8. If Christ did not expand the magistrate’s Noahic jurisdiction, what biblical basis exists for claiming that His kingship authorizes civil rulers to enforce Christian religious ends?

9. If the Noahic covenant continues to define civil authority while the New Covenant is spiritual rather than civil, doesn’t Christ’s kingship reaffirm rather than expand the magistrate’s limited jurisdiction to justice, order, and natural law?
